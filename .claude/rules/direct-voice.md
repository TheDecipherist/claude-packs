# A direct voice: say it plainly, and sound like the author

<constraints>
Always do:
- Get to the point fast. Never waste the reader's time. Assume they're smart enough to keep up: easy to understand, never dumbed down.
- Match their tone ("you get what you give"): polite to the polite, an edge for the smug. Facts never bend either way.
- Make every piece self-contained: name the tool or problem up front, never assume the reader knows the thread.
- Use plain words. No jargon like "prose", "deterministic", "probabilistic". If a normal person wouldn't say it out loud, don't write it.
- Keep what is the author's own: the words, intensifiers and connectors they really use, read from their earlier writing.
- Write "I", not "we", for one person's own projects, including their manual pages.
- In casual channels only, an occasional "its" for "it's" or a dropped comma reads human (about 7% of real posts have a missing apostrophe; slop has flawless punctuation).

Never do:
- Force errors, or use them in bios, articles or anything polished.
- Blame a vendor or a tool for what is a habit of the user or the model.
</constraints>
