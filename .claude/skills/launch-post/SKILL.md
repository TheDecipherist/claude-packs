# Launch and product posts

- **Lead with the argument, not the product.** Title is the claim, the
  first paragraph pays it off, the product shows up around paragraph five
  as the answer to the argument. A title that announces a product tells
  scrollers "ad" before they read a word.
- **No product name and no brand word in the title** when a topic word
  already implies it ("schemaless" already says Mongo and also pulls the
  Dynamo, Firestore and JSONB crowds). A tagline is not a title; it stops
  people the way a billboard does. The tagline goes as the LAST line,
  after the argument has landed.
- **Disclose once, plainly, in the first lines.** "I work at MongoDB" or
  "this is my own project and not a MongoDB product" when that's true.
  Never say the employer twice. Tim's own form: "(my employer, so check it
  yourself)".
- **Numbers only if you can defend them in the thread.** A benchmark that
  invites "so Operator got a skill file and the native driver didn't?"
  makes the thread about the benchmark. Hold contested numbers for a
  second post when they can be measured clean; that second post is its own
  launch.
- **Something to look at.** His best posts each had one concrete thing:
  4,681 vs 251 tokens, or the injection that almost got him. With numbers
  held back, a five-line code snippet does that job.
- **Early-development honesty beats a challenge close** for a launch: "I
  obviously havent been able to try every scenario so if you hit a bug or
  want something it doesn't do, open an issue" (an issue, not a PR; a PR is
  code), then one sentence giving the reader a job ("if Claude makes a
  mistake for you that isn't on the list, tell me, that's the next thing I
  fix").
- **Nothing negative about MongoDB, ever, in any Operator or MongoDB
  piece.** The AI writes tutorial-level Mongo because of the data it was
  trained on; the fault is the model's habits and the training data, never
  Mongo and never the native driver. "I got tired of seeing people bash
  Mongo for things that aren't Mongo's fault" is the register.
- **His own projects are "I", not "we",** including the manual pages that
  describe them.
