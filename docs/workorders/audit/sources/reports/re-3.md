## 3. Top gaps

Ranked for an unattended build of a synth from its firmware and its photos. Coverage is MISSING unless marked PARTLY.

1. **Identify the CPU and the load base from the image** (rows 22, 33, 34, 49). The kit starts at `re-toolchain` with the processors already named; the method (vector slots, word-addressed load base, `MOVW DP` operand map, positional scoring of both byte orders, V-series test, peripheral strings) lives only in the global `firmware-recon` skill. An unattended run on a new image has no way to pick a disassembler. Gain: new skill `identify-the-cpu`.
2. **Panel from the real unit** (rows 67, 68, 69, 71, 76; PARTLY). The bicolour-lamp grep, the test-mode switch and control count, the vector-overlay-first rule and the spec-first workflow exist only in the global `panel-from-the-real-unit` skill. The kit has `reproduction-or-original`, `render-the-extremes` and `analyse-reference-look`, but nothing that audits the panel against a hard count from the ROM, and `analyse-reference-look` ranks a photograph above a drawing, the reverse of row 26.
3. **Prove a layout by closure** (rows 2, 10, 37, 41; PARTLY). Chunk sizes sum to the file, count x stride lands on the next table, an offset chain closes link for link, the patch store fills the RAM, a manual-stated value is unique in the image, a name table beats its own shifted and shuffled controls. These are what turn a guess into a finding without a human; the kit has "find a table's start and end" only.
4. **Measure a filter and a law from outside** (rows 126, 127, 128, 129, 130, 139, 140, 141, 142). Divide by the wide-open render, never use the clamped maximum, subtract the source's band slope, check a second independent quantity, identify a byte by signature, write the prediction first. `measure-a-law` holds only "use a sine, two anchors". This is what lets a bounce against an emulator fix a law without a human ear.
5. **Term shapes for the cutoff sum** (rows 117, 118, 121, 122; PARTLY). `account-every-term` has the principle; the ten shapes (hardwired key follow, per-zone trim, velocity into EG depth, controller terms that sum at zero, invented term, collapsed terms, wrong one of a pair, gated term, coupling, CV tapped upstream) and the "read the adjustment procedure and the autotune patch" steps are what a run needs to find the 2.3-octave term.
6. **Vendor updater, unpacked-image strings and demo files as sources** (rows 19, 20, 35, 25). Open the maker's installer for device IDs and stage names, sweep the unpacked image for the loader's own messages, extract demo MIDI files; all missing from `gather-sources`.
7. **Rhythm versus timbre, per-sample slew of a synced time, and re-anchor only on a jump** (rows 156, 157, 160, 148, 155). The midi pack is otherwise complete; these five are what a unit that "syncs" still gets wrong in sound.
8. **Read the test before correcting a law, and say the scope of a disagreement** (rows 61, 29, 25 ordering as third source). Cheap rules that stop two common wrong "fixes"; none are in `two-sources-before-naming`.
9. **Do not simplify the modulation matrix; model the machine's envelope shape; check each shared field has its own reader** (rows 86, 110, 111, 104). Engine-side lessons that cost days and have no rule.
10. **Panel renderer rules and the native-asset conflict** (rows 73, 74, 75, 79, 80, 84). Sprite baked values, lossy cache keys, the controls probe reading all three places of a spec, per-instance field tables for frozen parameter blocks, and the unresolved clash between `ui-drawn-in-code` ("no sprites") and the native export design.

Also worth a line: row 44 (the reference-library toolchain shelf holds the per-CPU traps but is not in the packs, so a second user of the kit would not have it).
