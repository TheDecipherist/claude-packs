# Review: "How to create a patch" (edition 2026-11-09-a) for the kit

Read in full, 1,002 lines. Sections 0 to 2 are the method and the physics; section 3 is fifteen categories, each with the ear's needs, the measured numbers, the roles, the envelopes, the movement, the playing, the effects, the mistakes and house recipes; section 4 is the tables; section 5 the gate; section 6 the sources.

## Verdict

It belongs in the kit, as the body of the `design-a-preset` skill the research prompt already asks for (W4.2 there), and it answers two different questions that need two different files:

1. **"How do I make a pad / bass / bell?"** Sections 1, 2, 3 and 5. That is the preset skill plus its per-category references.
2. **"What does my synth need so it can make X?"** This is not in the document as a section, but every category's "what the ear needs", "engine and roles", "movement", "playing" and "effects" implies a capability list. Extracted once, that list is what the `workorder-writer` interview and the plugin template have been missing: answer 4.1 ("which sounds should it come with") and the engine, modulation and effects steps follow from it instead of from a guess. Appendix A below is that extraction.

Three things have to change for it to go in, and one thing stays out.

## What changes on the way in

**1. The 8,000-character cap.** The document is about 126 KB; `doctor` refuses a `SKILL.md` over 8,000 characters. So the skill is the method and the gate, and everything else is a reference file beside it, loaded by category:

```
packs/sound-design/skills/design-a-preset/
  SKILL.md                      sections 1 and 5, the vocabulary, how to load a category   (< 8,000)
  references/tables.md          section 4.1 to 4.4 as they are
  references/building-blocks.md section 2 as it is (waves, tuning, filters, envelopes, movement, playing, effects, levels)
  references/what-a-synth-needs.md   appendix A below (new)
  references/categories/<kind>.md    one per category from section 3: piano, keys, organ, strings, pad, lead, pluck,
                                     bass, brass, bell, arp, sequence-stab-chord, fx, drums, wind-vocal
```

The skill's description: "Use when designing, tuning, naming, reviewing or shipping a preset, patch, program, bank or INIT, asking how to make a kind of sound, or asking what a synth needs to make one." That is the claude.ai `preset-design-law` trigger plus the capability question.

**2. No person named, and the kit is for everyone.** The kit's own test refuses a file that names a person. the author's name appears throughout the source: the [EAR] tag, "what the author's ears said", "the author hears it", "the author on the first banks". Every one becomes "the author" or "the owner's ear" (the gate's last line: "The owner hears it."). The counts stay: "253 presets heard, 92 liked" is the evidence, the name is not.

**3. House engines versus the generic layer.** FIRESAW, FIREBLOOM, FIREBASS, FIRETINE, FIREKEYS, FIREWHEEL, BURN, SWELL, GLOW, rows, RIDE, space: these are Tabula and Stellix. The kit's `sound-design` pack is installed in every synth project, including a future one with none of them. Split by paragraph, not by file:

- **Kit (generic):** what the ear needs, the data, the filter choice, the envelope tables, the movement, the playing, the effects, the mistakes, the gate. Engine names generalised to what they are: "a seven-saw supersaw with detune, mix and drift", "a phase-locked body-and-sub bass engine", "a bit-shaped wave with motion", "a tine model with pickup position". `SWELL`/`GLOW` become "amp envelope" / "filter envelope" with the house names in brackets once, in the vocabulary.
- **Project (house):** the "Starting recipe" blocks in Stellix terms, and section 0's engine list by name. They are `stellix-sound-recipes.md` already, and they live in the Stellix and Tabula repos under `docs/`. The kit skill ends with: "then read this project's recipe file (`layout.json` `recipes`, default `docs/recipes/*.md`) for the machine's own engines". A project without one gets the generic recipe lines, which section 3 also has (the ANALOG and FM variants are generic).

The [PUB] cards (Juno pad, Moog bass, OB brass, Voodoo lead, Hoover, PPG bell, Acid line) are published starting points and stay in the kit as such. Machine names in a reference document are fine; the law that forbids them is about what ships, and the gate's rule 8 (no machine or brand name in a preset name) is where it bites.

## What stays out, and why

Nothing of substance. The one thing to drop is the engine-name table in section 4.5 as written: the mapping "engine to kinds" is house-specific. It is replaced by the generic version in Appendix A (source kind to category: harmonic, inharmonic, noise, physical model, phase-locked bass), and the house table goes with the recipes to the project.

## What the gate turns into (the structural part)

Section 5 is twelve rules a patch must pass. Half of them are numbers, and the kit's law is that a number becomes a check or a measurement, never a reminder. Measurable now, as a `prove-a-bank` tool the skill calls (same shape as `spread-probe` and `measure-reverb`, in `sound-design/tools/`):

| gate rule | measurement | exists |
|---|---|---|
| 4 it sits | -6 dBFS peak on a four-note chord at v100; bank within 1 LU; mono sum no hole over 3 dB in 100 to 500 Hz; everything under 120 Hz mono; nothing under 40 Hz; no clipping in the chain | partly (`bounce-a-plugin` renders; the measures are new) |
| 2 it plays | v40 and v110 differ by at least 12 dB and in spectral centre; wheel 0 and wheel 127 render different; cutoff tracks pitch at two points | new |
| 3 it moves | spectral difference between 0 to 2 s and 2 to 8 s on a held note with space off, above a floor; second note within tolerance of the first | new |
| 7 measured then frozen | level at three velocities; sync lands at 120, 90 and 140 BPM; tail below -60 dB in the stated time; no runaway feedback at any wheel position; first sound under 20 ms for pluck, bass, lead, keys | partly (`clock-probe`; the rest new) |
| 10 per kind | pluck/arp: delay subdivision not equal to the arp's; bass: no energy difference L/R under 120 Hz, no chorus under 150 Hz, glide off; pad: release at least the attack; bell: release equals decay; organ: velocity depth 0; lead: wheel moves pitch | new, from the patch file, not the audio |
| 11 each slot alone | a render per slot and a bank render at three tempos | `bounce-a-plugin` |
| 8 named to be found | `CATEGORY . Name`, three words, no machine or brand name | a lesson check on the bank files |
| 9 every module on has a reason | INIT equals the declared neutral; no preset equals the toolkit default envelope (the "Serum default left in" finding, 34 % of SW bells) | a check on the bank |
| 1, 5, 6, 12 | first note sells it; one identity and one imperfection named; something only this machine does; the owner's ear | the owner's, stated in the notes; not measurable |

The `presets-are-named-and-neutral` rule in the research prompt grows into the laws above that bind a bank: levels measured not typed, lows mono, release at least attack on pads, velocity audible, the wheel does something, delay off the arp's subdivision, glide off by default. One rule, one tool, two checks.

## What it gives the work-order writer

This is the part that changes the interview. Today question 4.1 asks "how many presets, in which categories" with a default of five categories. With Appendix A, the answer decides the synth: "pads, basses, bells" means the engine step must include an inharmonic source, per-slot filters and envelopes, a phase-locked sub, a chorus after the filter, a hall with pre-delay and low cut, a wheel assign to a slot's cutoff, and the gate measures all of it. A beginner never has to know that; they name the sounds and the order carries the capabilities. The plugin template's `[if ...]` markers come from this table rather than from a fixed list.

It also fixes the default: the document's liked shares say which categories a first bank should carry (sub, arp, acid, pad, atmosphere above 50 %; bass the hardest, lead and pluck in the middle). Default 4.1 becomes "8 pads, 8 plucks, 8 arps, 8 keys, 8 basses, 4 subs, 4 leads, 4 fx", with the note that bass takes the longest.

## Appendix A. What a synth needs to make each kind (extracted from the document)

Cross-cutting, every melodic synth: an INIT that is measured neutral; a filter with key tracking 0 to 100 % and types LP 12, LP 24, HP, BP, notch; per-slot (per-oscillator) amp envelope, filter envelope, filter and pan where the kind stacks roles; envelope curves LOG, EXP, linear; attack, decay and release ranges from 1 ms to 10 s; LFOs with a delay (vibrato comes in after 300 ms), free or synced per LFO, triangle, sine, square/step, random and S&H; velocity, wheel, aftertouch and pressure assignable to any control with depth; mono, poly, legato and unison modes, glide with a legato-only mode (never only "always"); drive placeable before or after the filter; delay with per-channel subdivision, loop HP and LP, feedback with a ceiling (no runaway at any wheel position), ducking from the dry; reverb with pre-delay, decay by family, send low cut, high damp and width under 100 %; chorus after the filter; a level meter that reads dBFS peak and LU on a four-note chord, and a mono-sum check.

| kind | the source it needs | the shaping it needs | the modulation it needs | the playing it needs | the effects it needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Piano** | a physical model or samples (a subtractive piano is "keys"); stretch tuning; per-note unison beating | two-stage release (fast drop then slow tail); damper on release; decay shortening up the keyboard | none by LFO | velocity to level (0.41 of range, the most of any kind) and to brightness; sustain pedal CC64 | reverb with pre-delay 50 ms or more; nothing else |
| **Keys / e-piano** | tine or reed model with pickup position and bark, or FM e-piano program, or pulse with PWM; per-key scatter or age | velocity to timbre by construction (pickup curve) or filter env amount with velocity; decay 1 to 3 s with a floor | tremolo LFO on amp (stereo), 2.5 to 5.6 Hz; filter tremolo; PWM 1 Hz | velocity to colour and level; wheel to tremolo depth; aftertouch to LFO depth | chorus (the keys' effect, 4 voices), phaser, plate or room 1.5 s |
| **Organ** | additive sines at footages (16' to 1'), drawbars; percussion (2nd/3rd, fast/slow, single-trigger); key click; leakage; scanner vibrato/chorus; tonewheel fold-back | a gate: instant on and off, no ADSR; velocity depth 0 | rotary: LFO on pan and gentle cutoff with slow/fast speeds and a 1 to 2 s ramp on the wheel | expression on wheel or pedal; no velocity | preamp drive that grows with keys down; room; slapback delay; chorus or scanner, never both |
| **Strings** | multi-saw groups with group drift (the section) or pulse with PWM (the machine); noise bow layer | LP 12; slow attack 100 to 300 ms with release at least the attack; filter env optional and shallow, or none (machine) | delayed vibrato 4 to 5 Hz; PWM 1.4 Hz; slow drift | velocity to cutoff and amp gently; legato without glide; aftertouch to vibrato depth | ensemble (slow, deep, three-phase) before the hall; hall 3 s with low cut 200 Hz |
| **Pad** | three slots with roles: body (supersaw or detuned saws or bit-shaped wave), air an octave up high-passed, ground sub mono unfiltered | per-slot filter, envelope and pan; LP 24 on body, HP on air; attack 250 ms to 1 s with release at least the attack; filter env longer than amp (colour moves after level) | 2 s movement (PWM, drift, engine motion) and 8 s movement (0.05 to 0.15 Hz on cutoff or blend); autopan on air only; a drift, never a vibrato | velocity never only volume (to filter env amount); wheel opens the air (slot cutoff or level); aftertouch to LFO depth | chorus after the filter; hall 2.5 to 4.6 s, pre-delay 20 to 40 ms, low cut 120 to 200 Hz, high damp, width 70 to 85 %; long dotted delay filtered |
| **Lead** | supersaw or detuned saws (lane 1); pulse or square an octave up high-passed (lane 2); optional sub floor; sync source for sync leads | LP 12 (keeps the band it lives in); 5 to 10 ms attack click; filter env with velocity; mono or two-voice legato with glide 30 to 80 ms legato-only | wheel vibrato 5 to 7 Hz with 300 ms LFO delay; PWM; drift | wheel to vibrato, no exceptions; velocity to filter env and amp; aftertouch to vibrato depth or cutoff; pitch bend ±2 | drive before the filter; delay first (1/8 dotted, filtered loop); plate on the send with low cut; reverb mix as a macro |
| **Pluck** | one saw or small-unison slot (never over 3 voices), or tine, FM mallet, bit-shaped wave; sub sine; optional noise or square click | sub-millisecond attack; filter env shorter than amp env (0.5 to 0.9x) with EXP curves; resonance 0.2 to 0.35; KT 100 %; first sound under 20 ms | pitch blip envelope (a few cents, gone in 50 ms); autopan slow | velocity to filter env amount 50 to 80 % and amp 30 to 50 %; wheel to cutoff or delay mix | delay 1/8 dotted, filtered loop, ducked from the dry (the pluck's effect); plate 2 to 3.5 s |
| **Bass** | phase-locked body and sub in one engine (or phase reset on both); body waves saw, square, reese, FM, growl, knock; a punch/knock pitch drop; sub sine an octave down | LP 24; KT 50 %; release 18 to 90 ms; filter env shorter than amp; mono legato; glide legato-only, off by default; accent (velocity over 100 adds to env amount) and slide for acid | wobble: synced square or step LFO on cutoff or osc mix at 1/4 or 1/8 with wheel to depth or rate; PWM on square bass; nothing on the sub | velocity to cutoff as much as amp, weight constant; wheel to cutoff; aftertouch to cutoff | drive after the filter on the body only; everything under 120 Hz mono, nothing under 40 Hz; no chorus under 150 Hz; reverb send low cut 300 Hz at most 0.1; sidechain input for house and techno |
| **Brass** | two detuned saw pairs (or supersaw for stabs); pulse for reed; pink noise breath layer; octave slot for trombones | LP 24 closed with a filter env whose attack (30 to 60 ms) is longer than the amp's (the swell); sustain around 0.6; filter and amp decays ride together | delayed vibrato 4.2 to 4.8 Hz; PWM 0.7 Hz | velocity to brightness (the blat) and amp; wheel to cutoff or vibrato; aftertouch to cutoff and amp (CS-80) | chorus; hall 2 to 2.5 s; a little drive before the filter |
| **Bell** | an inharmonic source: FM program (ratio 3.5, 1.4), ring modulation, hard sync with a pitch envelope, or bit-shaped wave with a strike; sine ping slot | release equal to decay (the ring never depends on the key); sustain 0; brightness envelope faster than amp (0.54x); decay shortening up the keyboard | partials beating (two slots 5 to 8 cents, or spread); slow shimmer ±3 cents at 4.5 Hz; autopan | velocity to strike brightness and amp; wheel to chorus depth or decay; aftertouch to LFO rate or pitch | hall or big 4 to 7 s with pre-delay 0 to 20 ms, low cut 90 to 150 Hz, width 0.6; doubling delay or 1/4 dotted; chorus or phaser; no distortion |
| **Arp** | a pluck voice; sub; optional second lane a fifth or octave up | an arpeggiator: 8 or 16 steps, rate 1/16 or 1/8, gate 30 to 90 % (never 100), modes up/down/as played/random, octave range 1 to 2, per-step velocity (accent map, never flat), swing 0 to 62, rests and ties, a key setting (not only C), hold/latch | filter LFO or row synced to 2 or 4 bars; blend stepped per bar; autopan slow on the second lane | velocity to filter env amount (the accent); wheel to cutoff or delay mix | delay on a different subdivision than the arp, L/R heads on different subdivisions, filtered, ducked; plate or hall 3 to 4 s; reverb and delay ducked from the dry |
| **Sequence / stab / chord** | mono bass voice or saw for sequence; voicing slots at chord intervals (0, +3/+4, +7, +10/+11, +5) each with own filter for chord and stab | a step sequencer with accents and slides, synced step LFO; inverted filter envelope (closes on the attack) for stabs and 33 % of HW chords; HP on a stab stack; short release | step rows on cutoff and pitch or osc mix; fast pitch shimmer 7 Hz on stabs | wheel brings delay and space in (sequence); velocity to cutoff and amp (chord, the most of any SW kind) | distortion at full mix; reverb dry-then-long-narrow-wash with no pre-delay (stab); delay 1/4 dotted at a high mix (chord) |
| **FX / texture** | noise with band, width and S&H rate; many-saw groups with heavy drift; bit-shaped motion; pitch-drop body for hits | BP and twin/formant filters at vowel pairs; HP then LP for risers; LP 24 with high resonance for zaps; KT off; filter env amounts large with times 1 to 5 s; pitch envelope ±24 st | two LFOs at unrelated rates; random shapes; rows over 4 to 8 bars on cutoff, pitch, delay feedback and reverb mix; a release-triggered sweep | wheel to cutoff or LFO depth (the rate of change); aftertouch to anything including ±12 st pitch | big reverb 6 to 8 s with pre-delay 60 to 120 ms (textures) or 0 (hits); long delay 1/2 with feedback 0.5 to 0.8 and a hard ceiling; flanger, phaser 0.08 Hz; bitcrush; a gate with reverb before it |
| **Drums** | sine or deep body with a fast pitch drop (-24 st in 30 to 60 ms) and a click; noise with band and width; fixed-pitch oscillators (KT off) for snare body and 808 hat ratios; FM metal for hats | pitch envelope on the oscillator; inverted filter env closing on the hit; sustain 0; velocity-switched decay (closed/open hat); staggered multi-burst for claps | none over time per hit; a slow LFO on noise band for loops | velocity to amp (92 %) and to noise brightness; KT off on kick, snare, hat; on for toms | per-slot DRY routing (the kick skips every effect); distortion at full mix; gated reverb keyed from the dry on snare; stereo widener on hats; a step-row kit with per-slot lanes and swing |
| **Wind / vocal** | triangle (flute), pulse 0.5 (clarinet), pulse 0.2 (oboe); pink noise breath; supersaw or pulse unison for choir | LP 12 with a filter env that arrives before the level (0.3x attack); release 11 ms (a breath stops); mono legato glide 15 ms; formant BP pairs morphing (vocal) | delayed vibrato 4.9 Hz; tremolo 6 Hz on aftertouch; overblow (velocity over 110 adds +12 st for 20 ms) | velocity to amp and cutoff; wheel to vibrato or the formant; aftertouch to tremolo | room 1.2 s (wind); hall 3.5 s with pre-delay 40 ms and chorus (choir) |

Reading the table top to bottom gives the capability list for any synth that claims a set of categories. Three capabilities appear in nearly every row and are the ones a first design most often leaves out: per-slot filter and envelopes (not one filter over the stack), an LFO delay so vibrato can arrive late, and a delay whose loop is filtered and whose feedback cannot run away.

## Open items for the port (decide in the kit order)

- Whether `references/categories/` keeps the house recipe blocks with the engine names generalised, or drops them to the project file entirely. Recommendation: keep the generic recipe (the ANALOG or FM variant each category has) in the kit, move the named-engine recipes to the project.
- The [EAR] evidence is one owner's; the kit is for everyone. Keep it, tagged as "the author's ear over seven rounds", because it is measured (253 heard, 92 liked) and the kit's own rule is that a measurement outranks an opinion.
- `prove-a-bank` needs a render path per preset and per slot. `bounce-a-plugin`'s offline VST3 host is the way; the gate's measurements are new code in `sound-design/tools/`, under 300 lines per file.
