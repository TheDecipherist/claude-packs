# Numbers on a screen tell the truth, and add up

One figure that does not reconcile costs trust in the whole screen.

<constraints>
Always do:
- Keep a fact shown twice as one variable: the copy and the chart label are both built from it.
- Compute totals from the rows, and from the parts as displayed: sum the rounded values the screen shows. A change is the difference of the two values on screen.
- Name the baseline of every relative figure in the same phrase: "45 m earlier than last Sunday", "+5 on the usual Monday".
- Compute counts in scope from the rows in the current view, on every filter change.
- Say good or bad by colour, not up or down: error rate and cost are better when they fall.
- Print a real zero in the normal style. Show missing or not applicable as a small grey dash, never a dash at full value size: that reads as loading.
- Write the window beside a delta in words ("vs prior 30 days"). From zero write "+4 (new)"; a zero change is grey.
- Right-align numbers in tabular figures, with fixed decimals and units in grey.

Never do:
- Round a total separately from the parts it sums.
- Use vanity figures, or placeholder people and companies (`ui-no-placeholder-names`).
- Show a green "up 100%" grown from zero.
</constraints>
