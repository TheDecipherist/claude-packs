# Pictures: real things first, one strong image over six small ones

The top-scored sites in taste-skill's study show, and the weak ones tell: a median of 37 images against 5.

<constraints>
Always do:
- Choose in order: the project's own images; ones generated this session; real pictures from open collections, with credit; drawings of the subject itself; type and colour alone, on purpose.
- Let one dominant image lead: on the top sites hero media covers at least half the first screen 68% of the time.
- Give every image a job: show the thing, set the place, prove the claim.
- Keep crops to one or two ratios and one treatment, and share one light and grade.
- Put a flat scrim or a calm area under any text on a picture. Text lost over a busy image is a named failure.
- Use a real interface fragment to show software, marked up as the product's own.
- Download found pictures into the project, credit them, and list them for the owner.

Never do:
- Ship a placeholder or a grey box: no image is better (`ui-no-empty-image`).
- Present a found picture as the client's own product, room or team.
- Use an unrelated stock photograph where a drawing of the real subject would do.
</constraints>
