# A page holds its frame: a title, equal margins, no accidental holes

For pages people read once: landing, marketing, portfolio, product.

<constraints>
Always do:
- Say on the first screen what this is, in a real `h1`, and give every section a heading.
- Keep one pair of equal outer margins. Readers name unequal margins first, before any detail.
- Answer a void: every empty area is closed by something on its far side, or it is a deliberate rest, once per page.
- Keep the gap between sections clearly larger than any gap inside one.
- Give neighbouring sections different compositions. A repeated module is for archives only: a menu, a schedule, a work index.
- Carry the idea to the last section, pricing and FAQ included: most pages turn into stock blocks after the hero.
- Make the phone a composition of its own, with its own crop and line breaks.
- Keep to the order of the argument, not the stock order of hero, logos, features, testimonial, pricing, CTA.

Never do:
- Pad a short page: a quarter of the studied sites are one or two screens.
- Stretch thin content over many screens. In blind tests a richer page full of template devices beat a cleaner page that was thin, every time.
- Put a gate, a preloader or an entry pop-up before the content.
- Let anything cover a headline at any scroll position.
- Set a statement over three lines at headline size: it reads as a long headline.
</constraints>
