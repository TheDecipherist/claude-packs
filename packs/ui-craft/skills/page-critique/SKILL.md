# Critique a rendered page (one job: find what a stranger would notice, and fix in the right order)

Make each pass on its own, on the screenshots at desktop and phone. App screens use the checks in `app-screen`.

<constraints>
Always do:
1. **One second, then five.** Glance away: there should be one answer, a word, an image, an object. "A website" means no focal point. Then five seconds as a stranger: what is this, who is it for, what do I do?
2. **Squint.** The page reduces to two or three large masses. Many similar masses means nothing leads.
3. **Cover the logo.** Could this belong to a competitor? Swap the name and the noun: an equally valid page has no idea. Does the idea reach the pricing, the FAQ and the footer?
4. **Edges.** Count the distinct left edges of text blocks. Two or three reused on purpose; six means things were placed one at a time.
5. **Space and frame.** Equal outer margins? Any section huddled on one side? A hole, or a rest? Any blank stretch over half a screen?
6. **Type.** Is the jump from body to headline decisive, three times or more? More than five sizes? Orphan words? Lines over 75 characters?
7. **Colour.** Name the proportions. An accent in ten places means nothing.
8. **Images.** Is every image doing a job? Do crops share ratios? Any grey box or fake screen?
9. **Sameness.** One word per section's layout. The same word twice in a row, or three times on the page, is the template showing. Past that: an image-and-text split at most twice in a row; a grid has as many cells as content; no section header with the headline left and a small paragraph right.
10. **Phone.** A composition of its own, or the desktop squeezed?
11. **Fullness.** Is each screen composed, or a narrow column with dead ground? An austere page is not a restrained one.
12. **Substance.** Does every section deliver its information at a readable size? Is the primary action a real button on the first screen?
13. **Collisions.** Anything on a headline or a button at any scroll position? Anything off the edge of the phone?
14. **The competitor.** Put the nearest competitor's name on it. Then name the one live thing the visitor can do.
15. **Cap the score.** Any placeholder caps content at 5. A blank band where a reveal failed caps usability at 5. No clear title, or anything covering a headline, caps usability at 6. An idea that fits anyone caps creativity at 6. Unequal margins or a huddled section cap design at 6.5.
16. **Fix in this order:** the idea, hierarchy, composition, typography, imagery, colour, detail, motion. Later items cannot rescue earlier ones.
17. **Read every visible string** one last time for broken grammar, fake wit and unclear referents; replace any cute or unclear one with a plain functional sentence.
18. **Three rounds at most:** critique, fix the top three, look again. Stop when nothing is left that a stranger would notice. Report the real score: never round up.

Never do:
- Refill a gap with an ornament: close it, enlarge what remains, or let a picture take the space.
- Trust a page because the checks passed.
</constraints>
