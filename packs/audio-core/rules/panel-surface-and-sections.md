# A panel is three surfaces, columns named at the foot, and sections switched by their own lamp

The house look, taken from the house reference plugin. A panel that follows it reads the same in every unit, so the user learns it once.

<constraints>
Always do:
- Use three surface levels only: the background (darkest, dark slate with a hint of blue and a subtle vertical gradient), the panel (rounded, a hairline top edge) and the header (a recessed bar). Displays sit in a near-black display surface.
- Give each functional block a column, named at its foot in large bold uppercase: the biggest text on the plate.
- Start each sub-section with a header bar: its title on the left, `ON` and a lamp on the right. The lamp is the module's kill switch: off, the output is bit-identical whatever its controls say (`module-kill-switch`).
- Put tabs in one row under a panel's title, the active one in orange. Show instances of one thing (LFO 1-4, ENV 1-4) in a selector grid with activity arcs.
- Tell instances apart by their header text or their grid cell: two filters are identical twins.

Never do:
- Add glows, shadows deeper than 2 units, or textures.
- Tell two instances apart by colour.
</constraints>
