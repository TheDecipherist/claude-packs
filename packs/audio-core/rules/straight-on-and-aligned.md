# A UI is drawn straight on, and everything aligns: basic UX comes before the picture

<constraints>
Always do:
- Draw the object straight on and flat, as if from infinitely far away. Depth comes from shading under one light, never from shifted shapes.
- Align everything horizontally and vertically. Controls in a row share one centre line, and controls in a column share one centre line.
- Treat whatever looks grouped as a group: the same size, the same spacing, shared edges, and even gaps.
- Give a control its size from its kind, never its instance. A table row holds position and parameter.
- Give a round control's parts one centre: cap, skirt, pointer and ticks.
- Keep text level, and mirror what is symmetrical: rack ears, slots, screw holes.
- Treat a photograph as evidence of the design, not as the design. Start from what is known of the object: a panel is a flat rectangle. Snap what you measure to the grid it implies.

Never do:
- Copy what belongs to the photograph: the camera's angle, the lens, the lighting, a bend, wear, a missing part. One bend moves everything near it.
- Keep a measured offset as design when it is smaller than the design's own spacing. It is error.
- Let a look gate reward a flaw. Mask what is damaged, and check alignment, sizes and centres on their own: a whole-picture score passes while every knob is wrong.
</constraints>
