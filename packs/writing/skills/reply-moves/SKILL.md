# Reply moves by situation

All replies: no preamble, conclusion first. (Posts are different: `post-voice`.)

- **Wrong AND smug:** soft-flex opener, receipt, flat correction. "I'm
  sorry but I think you need to read the docs again [link]. Skills are
  injected at session start..."
- **Wrong but polite:** skip the jab, open with the source or flat fact,
  calm. Calm is not agreeing; the false premise still gets corrected.
- **Polite AND sharp but framing wrong (the dangerous one):** acknowledge
  nothing false. Correction stated flat, one concrete consequence, stop.
- **True fact, false conclusion:** agree with the true fact plainly (that's
  not a concession), then refute the conclusion hard. Two reusable moves:
  the "it would already be baked in" move (if saying X were enough, Claude
  would already do X by default), and SHOW the specific instead of
  asserting it: give the actual list of concrete rules, and verify every
  item is airtight; one vague bullet undercuts the whole point.
- **Confidently misframing:** flat correction, conclusion first, one
  consequence, stop.
- **Genuine question (lost, not challenging):** straight into plain
  language, lead with an analogy, end on a crisp one-line distinction
  ("skills help, hooks enforce").
- **Disagreeing with an upvoted crowd belief:** open hard, stack flat
  declarative reasons, end with the fix. "I'm sorry but you are way off...
  Just give it a detailed description of what you want built and how."
- **Praise:** take it cleanly. "Thanks brother. Appreciate it." No
  deflecting, no over-thanking.

**Target register for replies (the author's own words, study it):**
> Hooks are the only thing that can cover you. There is no enforcement in
> a skill or in Claude.md for that matter. Hooks are the only thing that
> can stop Claude from reading, writing or piping a curl command through
> shell to steal all your personal information.

Conclusion first. Flat declaratives. Threat named plainly. Then it stops.
